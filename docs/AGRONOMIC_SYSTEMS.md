# GUMA Agronomic Systems Library

## Purpose

This library explains major agronomic production and management systems in accessible language. It is educational, not a prescription engine. Suitability depends on local climate, soil, topography, water availability and quality, crop/cultivar, machinery, labor, market, regulations and technical assistance.

## Taxonomy note

The catalog intentionally separates different levels of description:
- **Production systems:** how crops, livestock and trees are combined across space and time (rotation, succession, intercropping, ILP, ILPF, SAF).
- **Soil and crop-establishment systems:** how soil is prepared or protected (conventional tillage, no-till/direct planting).
- **Ecological approaches and standards:** principles and rule-governed approaches (agroecological and organic production).
- **Water and production environments:** irrigation, protected cultivation and hydroponics.

These categories are related but not interchangeable. For example, no-till is a soil-management system and can be used within several production systems; crop rotation can be part of no-till, ILP or conventional systems. Organic production is a regulated production framework, not a synonym for agroecology.

## Catalog entries

Each entry describes:
1. **How it works:** operational sequence and system logic.
2. **Typical flow:** simplified sequence of management operations.
3. **Potential strengths:** conditional benefits, not guaranteed outcomes.
4. **Limitations:** trade-offs, costs and constraints.
5. **What to monitor:** practical indicators and management checkpoints.

## Important distinctions

### No-till and conservation agriculture

No-till is not automatically equivalent to the full FAO concept of conservation agriculture. FAO describes three linked principles: minimum mechanical soil disturbance, permanent organic soil cover and crop-species diversification. The FAO framework includes locally adapted thresholds and practices; the GUMA page explains the broad concept and does not certify compliance.

### Integration and intercropping

A crop-forage mixture is not automatically ILP. Whether it constitutes integration depends on the role and use of components, including livestock activity and the planned production sequence. A cover crop grown only for mulch can be a consortium without being a crop-livestock integration.

### ILPF family

Brazilian technical references commonly distinguish ILP (agropastoral), ILF (silviagricultural), IPF (silvopastoral) and ILPF (agrosilvopastoral). The application focuses on ILP and ILPF but should expand to ILF and IPF as separate entries in a later iteration.
